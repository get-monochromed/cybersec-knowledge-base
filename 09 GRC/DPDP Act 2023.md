# DPDP Act 2023

## Summary

India's comprehensive personal data protection law, replacing the SPDI Rules regime under the [[IT Act 2000]]. Consent-centric, with obligations on **Data Fiduciaries** and rights for **Data Principals**, enforced by a **Data Protection Board**.

## Core concepts

- **Data Principal** — the individual whose data it is.
- **Data Fiduciary** — decides the purpose and means of processing (the controller).
- **Data Processor** — processes on behalf of a fiduciary.
- **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)** — notified class (volume/sensitivity/risk); extra obligations: DPIA, independent audit, DPO in India, algorithmic due diligence.
- **Consent** — free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, with clear affirmative action; must be withdrawable. Plus **legitimate uses** (e.g. employment, legal obligation, medical emergency).

## Obligations of a fiduciary

- Give a **notice** (purpose, data collected, rights, complaint route) in English or any Eighth Schedule language.
- Use data only for the stated purpose; **data minimisation** and accuracy.
- **Security safeguards** (reasonable technical and organisational measures) — the direct hook for [[ISO 27001]], encryption, access control.
- **Breach notification** — to the Board and to affected principals, in the prescribed form and manner.
- **Erasure** on withdrawal of consent or when purpose is served, unless retention is legally required.
- Children's data: verifiable parental consent, no behavioural tracking or targeted ads to children.
- **Consent Manager** registration for managing consents.

## Data Principal rights

Right to access information, correction and erasure, grievance redressal, and nomination (for death/incapacity). Right to complain to the Board.

## Penalties

Up to **₹250 crore** for failure to take security safeguards; ₹200 crore for breach notification failures; ₹50 crore for other obligations. (Schedule of penalties.)

## Practical notes

- Rules and operational details have been phased in — verify current commencement status and rule text before relying on specifics.
- No general "legitimate interest" basis like GDPR; consent and listed legitimate uses dominate design.
- Cross-border transfer permitted subject to notified restrictions.
- Overlaps with sectoral rules (RBI, SEBI, CERT-In directions) — check the strictest applicable requirement.
