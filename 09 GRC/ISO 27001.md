# ISO 27001

## Summary

The international standard for an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**: a documented, risk-driven, continuously improved system for managing security. Certifiable by an accredited body — which is why it is the most commercially useful security certificate for a company selling to enterprises.

## Clauses 4–10 (the mandatory part)

4. **Context of the organisation** — interested parties, scope of the ISMS.
5. **Leadership** — policy, roles, top-management commitment.
6. **Planning** — risk assessment and treatment, objectives, statement of applicability.
7. **Support** — resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information.
8. **Operation** — implement and control the risk treatments.
9. **Performance evaluation** — monitoring, measurement, internal audit, management review.
10. **Improvement** — nonconformity, corrective action, continual improvement.

## Annex A (2022): 93 controls in 4 themes

- **Organisational** (37) — policies, roles, threat intel, supplier relationships, incident management, BCP.
- **People** (8) — screening, training, disciplinary process, remote working.
- **Physical** (14) — perimeter, entry, equipment, cabling, disposal.
- **Technological** (34) — endpoints, access control, crypto, logging, vulnerability management, secure development.

## Core artifacts an auditor will ask for

- ISMS scope and context analysis.
- **Risk assessment** and **risk treatment plan**, plus a **Statement of Applicability** (which Annex A controls apply, why, and their implementation status).
- Security policy set, roles and responsibilities.
- Internal audit results, management review minutes, corrective actions.
- Evidence of operation: logs, tickets, training records, supplier reviews, test results.

## Certification cycle

Stage 1 (documentation/readiness) → Stage 2 (implementation audit) → certificate valid 3 years with annual surveillance audits → recertification. Nonconformities (major/minor) must be closed within agreed timelines.

## Honest assessment

- Certification proves a *process* exists, not that security is good. A well-documented ISMS can still have terrible controls.
- The risk-based approach is the valuable part: it forces you to justify controls and accept risk explicitly ([[Risk Management]]).
- Cost is dominated by the management system overhead, not the audit fee.
- Pairs naturally with [[NIST CSF]] (structure) and [[CIS Controls]] (technical implementation).
