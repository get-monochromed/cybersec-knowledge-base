# Criminology

The human side of security: what crime legally is, why people commit it, and why the explanations are contested. Narrower than [[Threats MOC]] — that section asks *who* attacks and with what, this one asks why human beings offend at all, and how you would know.

## The discipline

- [[Crime]] — legal definition and elements: act, prohibition, penalty, intent
- [[Criminology]] — the scientific study of crime; why single-cause explanations fail
- [[Scientific Theory]] — what counts as an explanation; verification, falsification, models

## Theories of crime

| Theory | Human nature | Offending explained by | Prevention style |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Classical Theory]] | free will | choice: pleasure weighed against pain | punishment set to deter |
| [[Deterrence Theory]] | free will | the perceived price of the act | certainty of detection, sentencing |
| [[Positivist Theory]] | determinism | antecedents — biological, then social | fix the antecedents |
| [[Conformity Perspective]] | conformist | conformity failing, not offending arising | (a starting assumption, not a programme) |
| [[Strain Theory]] | conformist, structurally constrained | gap between prized goals and legitimate means | widen legitimate means, or reduce goal pressure |
| [[General Strain Theory]] | conformist | disliked events and conditions generally | unresolved in the source |

Attribution and blame:

- [[Just-World Hypothesis]] — the lay theory that people get what they deserve, and what it does to our reading of offenders and victims

## Where it connects to security

- **Motive and insider risk** — [[Strain Theory]] and [[Insider Threat]]
