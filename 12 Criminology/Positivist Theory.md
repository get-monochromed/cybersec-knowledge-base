# Positivist Theory

## Summary

**Positivist theory** is closely aligned with **determinism**: free will cannot be the major explanation for behaviour, because **antecedents** — prior experiences or influences — determine how we will act. Human behaviour is governed by causal laws, and free will is undermined.

It is the direct opposite of [[Classical Theory]], and it is the position most contemporary criminology occupies, because most contemporary theories search for causes beyond free will.

## Two generations of antecedents

| Generation | Antecedents proposed | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Biological** | one's sex, race, even the size of the brain | Cesare Lombroso (1876) measured the skulls of dead and live prisoners and drew conclusions about their criminal tendencies |
| **Social** | negative early life experiences, lack of educational opportunity | later positivists |

Both generations look for antecedents. The source’s newer discussion distinguishes possible biological contributions from exclusive determination; see [[Biological Approach (Criminology)]], [[Sociological Criminology]] and [[Nonconformity Perspective]].

> [!warning] Unverified
> The earlier summary that positivists find a difference between offenders and non-offenders and treat it as the cause is an overgeneralisation. A measured difference alone does not establish causation; the newer excerpt describes biological factors as possible influences, not exclusive determinants.

## Deprecated

Both generations share the method: find a measurable difference between offenders and non-offenders, then treat the difference as the cause.

> [!warning] Unverified
> The source reports Lombroso's skull measurements without evaluating them. The biological-typology programme he founded is now treated as **historical** criminology, not current science; in particular the race and brain-size antecedents listed should not be read as causal claims. Recorded here descriptively because the source presents them that way, not as an endorsement.
>
> The source also gives no date for the *later* positivist shift to social antecedents, so the chronology across the two generations is incomplete.

## Consequences for prevention

Because behaviour is caused, prevention tries to **fix the antecedents**: support services for youth believed to be at risk of offending, early intervention, education and opportunity. This is the mirror image of [[Deterrence Theory]], which adjusts the price of the act instead of the person's disposition toward it.

Note the ethical trap that determinism walks into: if behaviour is caused, the offender is in some sense not responsible, yet the same logic licenses intervention *before* any offence occurs. Modern practice resolves this only partially, and usually by mixing positivist prevention with classical punishment — an incoherence the theories themselves do not resolve.

## Relevance to security

- Risk-based security is positivist: score the user, monitor the baseline, intervene on the antecedent. See [[Insider Threat]] and [[SIEM]].
- Vulnerability and patch prioritisation is the same bet applied to systems. See [[Vulnerability Management]].
- The failure mode is also the same: predicting *who* will offend is either weak (no reliable antecedent) or discriminatory (the antecedent is a proxy for group membership).
